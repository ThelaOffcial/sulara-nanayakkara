Where the instinct began

Sulara Nanayakkara picked up his first violin at seven. Ten years later, he stood at the front of the National Youth Orchestra — having already outplayed musicians twice his age.

The instinct that carried him there was equal parts discipline and curiosity, and it has never left him. It's the same instinct that now pulls Western concert tradition and Sri Lankan melody into the same phrase.

Two conservatories, one standard

That instinct took him abroad — first to the Royal Academy of Music in London, then to the Juilliard School in New York.

Under some of the instrument's most demanding teachers, he learned not just technique, but how to disappear inside a phrase — the difference between playing a note and meaning it.

A fusion he leads, not borrows

Sulara now leads the string section as Senior Head Violinist of Chamber Music Colombo, where Western classical tradition meets the modal colour of Sri Lankan raga.

His reading of Bach's Chaconne has filled halls with a particular kind of silence. In 2024, he toured the island to raise funds for young musicians who remind him of himself at seven.
